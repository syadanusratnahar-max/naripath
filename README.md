# NariPath — নারীপথ

**A bilingual (Bangla/English) digital companion that helps women in Bangladesh find support, discover opportunities and navigate life with confidence.**

Millennium Fellowship project prototype · Live demo: https://syadanusratnahar-max.github.io/naripath/

NariPath does not replace existing organisations or provide medical, legal, financial or emergency services. It makes the support systems that already exist easier to discover, understand and use.

## What it does

- **Services grid:** one colourful home screen of tools, like a mobile-banking app: Allowances, Applications, Work abroad, Near you, Eligibility, Documents, All services and My Path.
- **Allowances finder:** this year's (FY2026-27) amounts and rules for the old-age, widow and husband-deserted women, disability and Mother and Child allowances and the new Family Card. Two quick questions show *potentially relevant* allowances with matched and still-to-confirm criteria, how to apply (free), documents, timing and sources. Warns that a Family Card holder loses her other personal allowances.
- **My applications:** track any application (allowance, scholarship, loan, document) through Planning → Documents ready → Submitted → Under review → Approved / Not approved, with a follow-up date and calendar reminder. Stores only the name typed, the stage and the date, on the device.
- **Safe migration for work:** for women thinking of working abroad and their families: Probash Bondhu 24-hour line (16135, +8809610102030 from abroad), a 3-step agency licence check (BMET and BAIRA), migrant insurance and loan facts, warning signs, and before / abroad / coming-home checklists.
- **Every district covered:** for each of the 64 districts, NariPath lists five government offices that exist everywhere: the District Legal Aid Office (free lawyer, via 16699), the Women Affairs office (complaints of violence, child marriage, Mother and Child Benefit), the Social Services office (allowances), the Civil Surgeon's office (nearest government hospital and EPI centre) and the district administration portal, which lists every office's phone number. Each links to its official website and opens Google Maps directions. These district office listings show up in Explore and the navigator once a district is chosen, so the national lists stay short.
- **Support map:** a colourful, tappable map of all 64 districts, coloured by division and drawn inside the app (works offline, sends nothing). Tap a division to zoom in, then a district to see its offices with Call, Website and Directions; red dots mark One-Stop Crisis Centres. A **Coast** button highlights the 19 coastal districts.
- **Shelter finder** (map and Cyclones page): choose a district (coastal ones marked 🌊) to get a shelter search for each of its upazilas (494 upazilas) on Google Maps, plus whole-district, OpenStreetMap and district-portal links. No complete public dataset of shelter locations is openly available, so NariPath does not draw shelter pins; it reminds people that maps show only shelters someone has added and to confirm their official shelter with the Union Parishad, UNO office or CPP volunteers. "Find the nearest" buttons search Google Maps for a hospital, police station, Union Parishad, Social Services office or cyclone shelter. Every listing with an address also has a Directions link.
- **Mother and baby timeline:** enter a due date (or months pregnant) or a baby's birth date to see the national antenatal check-up schedule, postnatal check-ups, and the free EPI vaccine dates, plus danger signs and 16263. **Find care near you** has one-tap Google Maps searches (hospital, maternity care, Mother and Child Welfare Centre, community clinic, child doctor, vaccination centre, pharmacy, Upazila Health Complex), a district picker showing that district's hospital, Mother and Child Welfare Centre, One-Stop Crisis Centre and Civil Surgeon's office, and a short guide to which facility is for what. Information only: the dates are never saved and are forgotten when she leaves the screen.
- **Period Shikkha (পিরিয়ড শিক্ষা):** seven short, Bangla-first lessons about periods for girls, under Health & Wellbeing: what a period is, body changes, pads or cloth (change every 6 hours, wash with soap, dry in the sun), staying clean, pain and self-care, what people say and what is true, and when and where to get help (adolescent-friendly health corners, 16263, 109). One idea per card with a picture, big text and a Listen button; works offline. A true/false check can be taken before and after and is never saved. Respectful of families: myths are framed as "what people say" and corrected, not mocked. Includes tips for girls with disabilities and their carers. **No name, period dates or personal experiences are ever asked.** Every lesson is marked *draft* until a qualified health professional reviews it.
- **Period Shikkha group sessions (pilot):** for trained female teachers and health volunteers. Girls answer the same 7 questions before and after on a shared phone, numbered not named. The phone counts girls taking part, girls completing all lessons, average score before and after, misconceptions corrected (wrong before, right after) and schools or communities reached, with a box for sessions verified by a signed attendance sheet. Results can be copied as a summary and CSV.
- **Cyclones and floods:** 1090 early warnings and 999, what storm signals 1–11 mean, before / during / after checklists (documents in waterproof bags, go-bag, leave early, safety in shelters, relief lists, replacing lost documents with links to the document guides).
- **Six areas of support:** Education & Career, Health & Wellbeing, Rights & Legal Support, Personal & Digital Safety, Financial Independence, Everyday Life & Community.
- **What should I do next?** Start from your situation (study, work, health, rights, online problems, money) and get a step-by-step action plan linked to services and guides. Save it and tick off steps.
- **Support near you:** choose any of the 64 districts to see local services with address, contact, eligibility, cost, source, checked date and status, then nationwide services. Shows which districts have local information and which have not yet been researched.
- **Learn and understand:** five short guides (online safety, money and fraud, preparing for work, basic rights, reliable health information), each with sources.
- **Resource Passport:** every listing shows who it is for, cost, documents, contact, original sources, last-checked date and verification status.
- **My Path dashboard:** saved listings, plans and Action Paths with progress, and "continue where you left off". Optional, private (stored only in the browser) and clearable in one tap.
- **Relevance checker:** four tap-only questions show *potentially relevant* services, which answers matched and what still needs confirming. It never decides eligibility; answers are never saved.
- **Discreet mode:** hide saved items in My Path.
- **Get your documents:** step-by-step guides for birth registration, NID (new, correction, lost card), trade licence, and bank or mobile-money accounts, with fees, offices and official links.
- **Share with someone:** send a listing or guide by WhatsApp, SMS or copy, with its source date and link. Nothing about the user is included.
- **Reminders:** set a date on any plan or Action Path and add it to Google Calendar or a calendar file, so the phone does the reminding.
- **Display settings (Aa):** larger text, high contrast, simple mode (main actions only, bigger buttons) and reduced motion.
- **Screen readers (TalkBack, VoiceOver):** every screen passes an automated axe-core check (WCAG 2 AA, including colour contrast) in Bangla and English. When the page changes, focus moves to its heading and the tab title updates, so the new page is announced; after a tap, focus returns to the same control. A skip link jumps to the main content, buttons have spoken names, pictures are hidden from the screen reader, and the map has a district list for people who cannot see it. Period Shikkha lessons and quiz questions are read out as each new card appears. Not yet tested with blind users on real phones.
- **Works offline:** installable from the browser. After one online visit, every page (guides, allowances, safe migration, saved plans and applications, helpline numbers) opens without internet, including after an update. Calling still needs a phone signal.
- **Report a problem:** wrong phone number, broken link, closed service, changed eligibility, wrong cost. Reports stay on the device in this prototype and can be copied to send to the team.
- **Read aloud** in Bangla or English (on-device voices preferred), **tap-to-call** and **copy number** buttons.
- **Information Gap Finder:** an admin view that separates information *not yet researched* from information *checked and not publicly available*, with coverage across all 64 districts.

Records are Type A (public source). Office websites follow the Bangladesh National Portal pattern (for example `dwa.kushtia.gov.bd`, `dss.bogra.gov.bd`); some still use older spellings such as bogra, comilla, jessore and barisal. Listings include the 109 and 999 helplines, Police Cyber Support for Women, Shastho Batayon 16263, the government legal aid helpline 16699, District Legal Aid Offices, BLAST, One-Stop Crisis Centres, SME Foundation's WE-HELP, Bangladesh Bank women-entrepreneur refinancing, UGC merit scholarships, SICIP skills training, the Probash Bondhu call centre, BMET and Probashi Kallyan Bank. Every record and allowance lists its sources in the app.

Allowance amounts, rules and dates come from news reports of government decisions, not from NariPath. Each allowance card shows its last-checked date and names the office that has the final word.

Every listing describes a real service. None is fictional, and no provider has confirmed its listing yet.

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Public-source information | Found in a public source |
| Awaiting provider confirmation | Direct confirmation requested, pending |
| Provider-confirmed | The provider confirmed it, with reviewer and date |
| Needs review | May be out of date (set automatically when the review date passes) |
| Demo record | Synthetic test data (none in this version) |

A source URL alone never makes a listing provider-confirmed.

Information can change. Always confirm with the provider before acting.

## Run it

Open `index.html` in any browser, or visit the live demo. To host your own copy, enable GitHub Pages (Settings → Pages → Branch: `main`, folder: `/ (root)`).

## Design

Light "alpona morning" theme: blush-white paper with a faint kantha-stitch pattern, violet-to-jamdani-pink accents, and marigold, teal and leaf-green service tiles. The home banner has drifting colour and a slowly turning alpona flower; service tiles pop in once per visit, tool banners have a soft sheen, call buttons pulse gently, and finishing a checklist step or moving an application forward sets off a small confetti burst. All motion stops when the phone's "reduce motion" setting or NariPath's own Animations setting is off, and nothing you tap moves while you aim at it. The app always opens in the light theme.

## Privacy

- **Nothing is sent to NariPath.** There is no NariPath server, account or login. Saved items, plans, reminders, display settings, problem reports, safe-migration and disaster checklist ticks and tracked applications are kept in the browser's local storage on the user's device, and "Clear my data" removes them.
- **Period Shikkha asks nothing personal.** Only which lessons were finished is saved. Quiz answers are not saved. Group sessions store the school or community name, the date and numbered, anonymous answers on the facilitator's phone.
- **Mother and baby dates are never stored.** They are kept in memory only while that screen is open.
- **Application titles are typed by the user** and saved exactly as written on the device, so the app asks people not to enter names or ID, account or phone numbers. NariPath does not ask for or store ID photos, health records or money.
- **Third-party requests:** the app, fonts and icons all load from the same address (GitHub Pages, which like any host sees the visitor's IP address). There are no analytics, advertising or tracking scripts and no Google Fonts requests. The user leaves NariPath only by tapping a link: a phone call, a WhatsApp or SMS share, Google Calendar, Google Maps directions or search, or a source website. The district map is drawn from data inside the app, so viewing it sends nothing anywhere and NariPath never reads the user's location. On some phones, read-aloud uses an online voice service that receives the text being read.
- NariPath is not an emergency service. Personal safety alerts (check-in timer, trusted-contact alerts) are described on the About page only as possible future work; they would need a native Android app, an SMS provider, weekly delivery tests and safeguarding review before being built.

## Roadmap

Period Shikkha pilot plan:

1. A qualified doctor or nurse and two female teachers review every lesson; the draft label comes off only after review. Record a female health worker reading each lesson in Bangla to replace the phone's synthetic voice.
2. Pilot in 2–3 schools or communities in one upazila, with school permission and parents informed. Sessions are voluntary and girls may skip any question.
3. Report only verified numbers: girls completing modules, before/after knowledge scores, misconceptions corrected, and schools or communities reached with signed attendance sheets.
4. Evidence base: BAHWS 2019-20 found only 30% of unmarried girls knew about periods before their first one, and 73% want to learn about puberty.

General roadmap:

1. Test with women from different backgrounds and districts.
2. Connect the Supabase database and shared problem reports.
3. Expand district coverage and confirm direct contacts with providers (Type C).
4. Only after safeguarding review: a native Android app for safety alerts, with weekly delivery tests.
